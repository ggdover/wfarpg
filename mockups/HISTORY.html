Aim with the mouse

Want

New

  • You can run one way while aiming and attacking another

What this means for animation

New

The character can face one way while moving another, so a forward run alone won't do. It needs movement animations for each direction relative to where it faces: forward, backward, and strafing left and right, blended for the diagonals.

That raises the number of animations we have to make, so it's worth settling early.

  • Gravel, pebbles and the stones on the paths in the same tan tones, so they read as texture
  • Small fallen leaves in yellow, orange, brown and the odd red, scattered over the grass and the paths in loose drifts
  • No more brown needle litter around the trees: the grass runs right up to the trunks
  • The Props and details and Ground vs. props cards updated to match

Found along the way

  • The scene's colour transform washes out light colours, so the first tan came out pale grey-beige; the colours going in had to be warmer and more saturated to come out tan
  • Leaves of real size were only two or three pixels long at the default camera, so they are drawn a little larger than life
  • Tallmo's square is still the darker packed dirt, so it stands out a little against the lighter roads

Ground vs. props

Want

Reworded

  • Ground: a smooth, flat surface with painted textures: moss, grass patches, gravel on dirt paths, scattered fallen leaves
  • Under trees: grass, no brown patches
  • Props (trees, bushes, rocks): faceted low-poly shapes

Props and details

Want

Reworded

  • Rocks: chunky, with uneven faces of mixed shapes and sizes, not lots of small triangles
  • Tree trunks: one piece, straight or gently leaning
  • Paths: winding, uneven width, curved forks, and worn into the grass: no hard edge, the grass thins out toward them. Light tan dirt, with gravel and pebbles in the same tones
  • Berries: grouped in patches, sitting on the bushes
  • Avoid: blocky purple flower clumps

Worn paths, version 2

New

  • Lighter paths: light tan dirt, between wheat and desert sand
  • Stones blend in: the gravel and pebbles are the same tones as the dirt, so they read as texture rather than grey spots
  • Fallen leaves: small yellow, orange and brown leaves on the grass and the paths, thicker in loose drifts
  • Grass under the trees: the brown needle litter is gone, so the grass runs right up to the trunks
